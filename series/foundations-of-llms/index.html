Foundations of LLMs

The core ideas behind large language models, following the first chapter of the book.

The first series on this site. It covers the foundations of large language models, following the first chapter of The Hitchhiker’s Guide to Agentic AI. New parts are added as I work through the chapter.

Prerequisites: some Python, and basic linear algebra (vectors, matrix products, eigenvalues). The companion pages linked from each article go deeper into the math and can be skipped on a first read.

Part Title Description
1 Tokenization and Byte-Pair Encoding: How LLMs Turn Text into Numbers Why language models don’t read characters or words, how BPE builds a vocabulary by merging frequent pairs, and what that means in practice.
2 From Token IDs to Vectors: How LLMs Embed Text What the embedding layer actually is, where its vectors come from, how weight tying works, and why the geometry of embeddings matters.
3 Anisotropy and Whitening: Fixing the Geometry of Embeddings What anisotropy is, what the evidence says about BERT, ELMo and GPT-2, and how whitening (and Whitening-k) reshapes embeddings for similarity search.
4 Scaled Dot-Product Attention, Step by Step How a token builds a new representation as a weighted mix of all the tokens around it: the intuition, the shapes, a worked example, and the reason for the √d_k scaling.
5 Masks, Cross-Attention and the Original Transformer The causal mask, teacher forcing and the right shift, cross-attention, and how the original encoder-decoder Transformer became the decoder-only architecture behind modern LLMs.
6 Multi-Head Attention: Several Views of the Same Sequence How multi-head attention splits one wide attention into several narrower ones, why that costs the same, what the original ablations showed, and how to read claims about what heads learn.
7 The Cost of Attention: n², the KV Cache, GQA and MLA Where attention’s quadratic cost comes from, what the KV cache is and how big it gets, how GQA and MLA shrink it, and a map of the long-context fixes (FlashAttention, sliding windows, sparse and linear attention, cache eviction).
8 Positional Encoding Basics: Why Transformers Need Positions Attention has no sense of order. Sinusoidal, learned, relative-bias and ALiBi encodings are four ways to add it.
9 Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) RoPE encodes position by rotating queries and keys, so attention scores depend on content and relative distance only. Intuition, math, code.
10 Stretching RoPE to Long Contexts Why RoPE models break past their training length, and how position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling, dynamic scaling and YaRN fix it. Plus: long context is not the same as long-context use.
